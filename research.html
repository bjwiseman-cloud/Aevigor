Daily status

A Go, OK, Caution or Go easy call each morning from how you feel, your HRV trend and your sleep, each compared with your own normal. Endurance training only.

  1. How you feel (fatigue, soreness, sleep quality, stress, mood) is weighted most, scored against your 4-week normal. [1]
  2. HRV uses the 7-day average of ln(HRV) against a 60-day baseline with ±0.5 and ±1 SD bands, instead of single-day readings. [2][3][4][5][6]
  3. Resting heart rate only confirms the other signals, because on its own it is a weaker marker of training status. [4]
  4. Under 6 hours of sleep lowers the status one step. [7][8]
  5. The exact cut-offs (0.5 and 1 SD, the order of signals) are coaching choices built on the research above. COACHING A

Sickness check

The daily check-in asks "Feeling sick?". Symptoms below the neck set Go easy; mild symptoms above the neck lower the status one step.

  1. Hard exercise during illness, especially with fever or chest symptoms, is discouraged. [9]
  2. The "above or below the neck" rule is a widely used coaching and sports-medicine rule of thumb, not a tested threshold. COACHING PRACTICE

Weekly check

Flags a run of low HRV days as a sign to deload. 3+ days of low 7-day HRV = Deload; with high resting HR too = Deload - strong; 1-2 days = Watch.

  1. A sustained drop in the 7-day HRV trend, not one bad day, is used as the warning sign. [3][2][10]
  2. Overreaching is recognised by a pattern over days, together with how you feel. [11][12]

Trends and HRV variation

Charts of feel, HRV, sleep, resting HR, daily effort and load, each with a 7-day line and your normal range. Low HRV with day-to-day variation (CV) outside its usual range for 3+ days is flagged.

  1. HRV day-to-day variation (CV) adds information beyond the average. [3]
  2. Sleep bands (7-9 h target, 6-7 h borderline, under 6 h short) match intervals.icu. [7]COACHING B

Training load

Fitness (42-day), Fatigue (7-day) and Form per sport, planned vs actual load, ramp rate, and a countdown to your next test, race or event.

  1. Fitness, fatigue and form follow the impulse-response model. [13][14][15]COACHING A
  2. Load is power-based for rides, pace-based for runs (heart rate as backup), and session RPE (effort × minutes) for lifting and badminton. [16][17]COACHING ACOACHING C
  3. The acute:chronic workload ratio is not used as an injury alarm, because of known flaws. [18]
  4. Ramp rate guide (3-7 a week ideal, over 8 too fast) and Form zones (match intervals.icu). COACHING DCOACHING B
  5. Aim for Form +5 to +15 on test days and before races, so you arrive fresh; a short taper improves performance. [19][20]COACHING ACOACHING D
  6. The only load alert is actual load more than 20% above plan over 7 days. [12]

Cycling performance

Power curve, FTP and VO2 tests, efficiency factor, decoupling and durability.

  1. Power curve: best power from 1 s to 3 h, last 90 days vs all-time best. [21][22]
  2. FTP = 95% of a 20-minute test. Useful for tracking, but it may not match a lab threshold for every rider, so the app compares you only with yourself, using one test method. [23][24][25][26]COACHING A
  3. Efficiency factor (power ÷ heart rate) on steady zone 2 rides, and decoupling (how much power per heartbeat drops from the first half to the second). [27][28]COACHING ACOACHING D
  4. Heat, dehydration and fatigue raise heart rate at the same power (cardiovascular drift), so rides are checked for these conditions. [29][30][31]
  5. Caffeine is not tracked: it does not change heart rate at steady submaximal effort. [32]
  6. The 1.05 variability limit and 5% decoupling line are coaching guides. COACHING ACOACHING D

Durability

A 5-minute max after about 1500 kJ of zone 2 riding, shown as a % drop from your fresh 5-minute best, with a target of 5% or less.

  1. Durability (how well you hold power when tired) is a separate part of fitness. [33]
  2. Fitter riders and polarised training show smaller drops; harder riding beforehand causes bigger drops. [34][35][36]
  3. Fatigued power is measurable and repeatable, and kJ of prior work is a practical way to set the fatigue level. [37][38]
  4. The 1500 kJ marker and 5% target are coaching choices for a non-pro rider. COACHING PRACTICE

Running performance

Pace, threshold pace, heart rate vs pace efficiency and decoupling.

  1. Pace zones and threshold pace. COACHING C
  2. Heart rate vs pace drift uses the same cardiovascular drift research as cycling. [29][30]COACHING D

Strength training

Estimated 1-rep maxes for powerlifting, volume load (sets × reps × weight) for bodybuilding, and reps in reserve (RIR) blocks.

  1. Reps in reserve as a way to rate how hard a set was, and RPE-based loading works as well as fixed percentages. [39][40][41]
  2. Autoregulated progression (APRE): adjust the weight from the reps you actually get. [42][43][44]
  3. More weekly sets give more muscle growth, up to a point, so volume load is tracked. Strength responds to heavy loads and fewer sets. [45][46][47][48]
  4. Estimated 1-rep max from a rep set. COACHING E
  5. Lifting does not change the daily status; it counts toward load through session RPE. [16]

Badminton

Session load (effort × minutes), time on court and heart rate during play.

  1. Badminton is high-intensity and intermittent, with heart rate often above 90% of max, so heart rate is tracked. [49]
  2. Session RPE captures load in sports without power or pace. [16]

Longevity

Why Aevigor focuses on endurance fitness, strength, cycling, running and racket sports.

  1. Higher cardiorespiratory fitness is linked to lower long-term mortality. [50][51]
  2. Racket sports, cycling and aerobics were linked to lower death rates in a cohort of 80,000 adults. [52]
  3. Cycling to work was linked to lower cardiovascular disease, cancer and death. [53]
  4. Muscle-strengthening activity, and grip strength, are linked to lower mortality. [54][55]
  5. Mixing endurance and strength training (hybrid training) still builds strength and muscle; keeping hard leg sessions apart from hard rides limits interference. [56][57][58]
  6. These are observational links: they show association, not proof of cause. COACHING PRACTICE
